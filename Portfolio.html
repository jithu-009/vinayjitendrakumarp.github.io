99,441 orders · 27 states SQL

Target Brazil: e-commerce growth, delivery and freight

Analysed two years of Target's Brazil e-commerce data in BigQuery to find where the business is growing and which regions are slow or expensive to serve.

Question
Where is order value growing, and which states pay the most in delivery time and freight?
Data
99,441 orders from 4,119 cities in 27 states, Sep 2016 – Oct 2018, across customers, orders, order items and payments tables.
Method
Multi-table joins, CTEs, LAG for year-over-year growth, CASE for time-of-day buckets and DENSE_RANK to rank states.
Findings
  • Payment value grew 137% from Jan–Aug 2017 to Jan–Aug 2018, from 3.67M to 8.69M.
  • Delivery takes 8.3 days on average in São Paulo but 29 days in Roraima. Average freight per item is 15 vs. 43.
  • Orders in five remote states arrive 16–20 days before the promised date, so the delivery estimates are too cautious, not the logistics too fast.
Recommendation
Recalibrate delivery estimates for remote states so customers see realistic dates.
Tools
SQL · BigQuery

Query

WITH AVG_DEL_TIME AS (
  SELECT c.customer_state,
         ROUND(AVG(TIMESTAMP_DIFF(o.order_delivered_customer_date,
                                  o.order_purchase_timestamp, DAY)), 2) AS avg_delivery_time
  FROM `Target.customers` c
  JOIN `Target.orders` o
    ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
  GROUP BY c.customer_state
)
SELECT customer_state, avg_delivery_time, 'Highest' AS category
FROM (
  SELECT customer_state, avg_delivery_time,
         DENSE_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY avg_delivery_time DESC) AS rnk
  FROM AVG_DEL_TIME
) t1
WHERE rnk <= 5
UNION ALL
SELECT customer_state, avg_delivery_time, 'Lowest' AS category
FROM (
  SELECT customer_state, avg_delivery_time,
         DENSE_RANK() OVER (ORDER BY avg_delivery_time ASC) AS rnk
  FROM AVG_DEL_TIME
) t2
WHERE rnk <= 5;
550,068 purchases · 5,891 customers Confidence intervals

Walmart Black Friday: is the spending gap real?

Used the Central Limit Theorem and confidence intervals to test whether differences in Black Friday spend by gender, marital status and age are real or noise.

Question
Do men and women really spend different amounts per purchase, and how much data is needed to be sure?
Data
Walmart Black Friday transactions: 550,068 purchases by 5,891 customers across 3,631 products, with no missing values.
Method
95% confidence intervals for mean spend by group, and simulated sampling distributions at n = 300, 3,000 and 30,000 to show how precision changes with sample size.
Findings
  • Men spend $9,438 per purchase and women $8,735, a gap of about $703. The 95% intervals ($9,422–9,453 and $8,709–8,760) don't overlap, so the gap is real.
  • Marital status barely matters: within each gender, married and unmarried shoppers differ by only $40–130 per purchase.
  • The interval is about $1,150 wide at n = 300, $110 at n = 30,000 and $31–51 on the full data.
Recommendation
Use bundles and multi-buy offers aimed at female shoppers to raise their spend per purchase.
Tools
Python · Pandas · Seaborn
10,886 hourly records Hypothesis testing

Yulu: what actually drives e-bike demand

Tested which factors significantly change Yulu's electric-cycle rentals, to help plan fleet size and pricing after a revenue dip.

Question
Do working days, weather and season really change rental demand, or do they only look like they do?
Data
10,886 hourly rental records with season, weather, working day, temperature, humidity and wind speed.
Method
2-sample t-test (working day), one-way ANOVA (weather, season) and chi-square test of independence (weather × season), with Shapiro-Wilk and Levene's tests to check assumptions.
Findings
  • Working days don't change average hourly rentals (p = 0.23), though weekdays show sharper commute-hour spikes.
  • Weather and season both change demand significantly (p < 0.001). Rentals drop sharply in rain or snow, and spring is the weakest season.
  • Weather depends on season (chi-square, p < 0.001), so demand forecasts should use both together.
Recommendation
Schedule fleet maintenance in spring, and use weather forecasts to adjust fleet size and field staffing.
Tools
Python · SciPy · Pandas · Seaborn
180 customers · 3 models Customer profiling

Aerofit: who buys which treadmill

Profiled buyers of Aerofit's three treadmill models so the company can recommend the right model to new customers.

Question
What separates buyers of the entry-level, mid-range and premium treadmills?
Data
180 customers with age, gender, income, education, marital status, self-rated fitness and planned weekly miles.
Method
Descriptive statistics, IQR outlier checks, income and miles by model, correlation heatmap and pairplots.
Findings
  • The entry-level KP281 is 44% of units sold, the mid-range KP481 33% and the premium KP781 22%.
  • KP781 buyers earn far more: median income near $77,000, against under $50,000 for the two cheaper models.
  • KP281 and KP481 buyers have nearly identical income profiles, so demographics alone can't tell them apart.
Recommendation
Recommend the KP781 to customers planning 150+ miles a week, and pitch the two cheaper models on features rather than demographics.
Tools
Python · Pandas · Seaborn
8,807 titles EDA

Netflix: what the catalog is made of

Cleaned and explored Netflix's catalog to find patterns in content type, genre, country, rating and growth over time.

Question
What does Netflix's catalog look like, and how fast did it grow?
Data
6,131 movies and 2,676 TV shows with cast, country, genre, rating, duration and date added.
Method
Parsed dates, filled missing text fields, and split multi-value columns (cast, country, genre) into one row per value before counting.
Findings
  • Movies make up about 70% of the catalog.
  • Titles added per year rose from under 100 in 2015 to about 2,000 in 2019.
  • TV-MA and TV-14 are the most common ratings, and most movies run 90–110 minutes.
